Smooth curves in mixed models

When a response changes with a covariate along a curve whose shape is not known, semireg() fits it as a penalized spline inside a linear or generalized linear mixed model (Ruppert, Wand and Carroll 2003). The spline’s wiggly part is a random effect, so the amount of smoothing is estimated by REML along with the other variance components, and the usual mixed-model tools apply. Smooths are written in the model formula, as for mgcv::gam(), next to fixed and lme4 random terms. semipred() predicts the curves with confidence bands; predictmeans() gives means and comparisons of factor levels with the smooth included.

Data and model

The Dialyzer data (package nlme) give the ultrafiltration rate of 20 dialyzers at seven transmembrane pressures, at two blood flow rates (QB, 200 or 300 dl/min).

library(predictmeans)
library(ggplot2)      # autoplot()
data(Dialyzer, package = "nlme")
Dialyzer <- as.data.frame(Dialyzer)

The rate rises with pressure and levels off. The model has a curve for each blood flow rate, s(pressure, by = QB), and a random intercept and slope for each dialyzer:

fm <- semireg(rate ~ s(pressure, by = QB, bs = "os", k = 4) + (pressure | Subject),
              data = Dialyzer)

bs = "os" is the O’Sullivan penalized spline (Wand and Ormerod 2008) with k interior knots; any mgcv basis can be used instead (the default is a thin plate spline). Each curve is split into a straight line, whose terms (QB + QB:pressure) are added to the fixed effects automatically, and a smooth departure from it, a block of random effects with its own variance.

Written Meaning
s(x) one curve
s(x, by = f) a curve per level of f, a variance each (id = 1: one shared)
s(x, by = z), z numeric varying coefficient
s(x, f) common curve + a deviation curve per level of f
s(x, f1*f2) common curve + deviations for f1, f2, f1:f2, a variance each
s(x1, x2), s(x1, x2, bs = "ztps") surface
t2(x1, x2) tensor-product surface (covariates on different scales), a variance per penalty; te() is fitted as t2()
s(x, bs = "user", xt = list(basis = f)) a basis of your own, f(x) (checked before the fit)
(s(x, k = 10) \| g) random curve per subject: (x \| g) + a spline deviation

The result prints a summary and holds its tables in the layout of predictmeans(): as.data.frame(cur) (or cur$means) has estimate, std.error, df, conf.low and conf.high with the pressure under its own name. Nothing is drawn unless asked (autoplot() as here, or plot = TRUE). The 1.x table is still in cur$pred_df.

The band is pointwise: at each pressure it covers the true curve with 95% probability (level = 0.05). Before version 2.0 these limits were 90% limits; level = 0.1 gives them. They are t limits with the Satterthwaite df of each point (column df), from the variance components of the design (here the dialyzers) and the residual.

A simultaneous band covers the whole curve at once with 95% probability, so it is wider. Use it to judge the shape of the curve over its whole range:

sim <- semipred(fm, "QB", "pressure", band = "simultaneous")
p <- sim$means
head(data.frame(pressure = p$pressure, df = p$df, pointwise = qt(0.975, p$df),
                simultaneous = (p$adj.conf.high - p$estimate) / p$std.error))

The critical values come from 10,000 draws of the estimates’ distribution (a multivariate t), with a fixed seed, so the band is the same each time. In the table the pointwise limits stay in conf.low/conf.high and the simultaneous band is in adj.conf.low/adj.conf.high, as the adjusted limits of predictmeans().

Where do the curves differ?

contr gives the difference between two curves, here 300 minus 200 dl/min, with its band. Where a simultaneous band excludes zero, the curves differ, with the error rate controlled over the whole range of pressures. Here the higher blood flow rate gives a higher ultrafiltration rate from a pressure of about 1.2 upwards:

dif <- semipred(fm, "QB", "pressure", contr = c(2, 1), band = "simultaneous")
dif$significant

#> 1 1.204694 3.03         >

Which smooths: sm_term

By default the curves use every smooth of the fit. sm_term picks some: a term as written in the formula, a smooth as labelled, a covariate (all its smooths) or a block name (names(fm$u_lst)). settings$sm_term records the blocks used:

semipred(fm, "QB", "pressure", sm_term = "s(pressure, by = QB)")$settings$sm_term
#> [1] "s_pressure_QB200" "s_pressure_QB300"
semipred(fm, "QB", "pressure", sm_term = "s(pressure):QB300")$settings$sm_term
#> [1] "s_pressure_QB300"

A smooth of another covariate adds a constant to the curves; the result’s advice says so (see the additive model below).

Slopes and curvature: deriv

deriv = 1 gives the slope of each curve along the covariate, deriv = 2 its curvature, with the same bands and df. Where the band lies above zero the rate still rises with pressure; the ranges are printed with the result and kept in significant (here with a simultaneous band, which holds over the whole range at once). The table slp$slopes adds the t test of slope = 0:

slp <- semipred(fm, "QB", "pressure", deriv = 1, band = "simultaneous")
slp$significant

contr with deriv compares the slopes of two curves. The derivatives are those of the whole fitted curve (fixed and smooth parts), for any basis, and for the O’Sullivan basis equal the exact derivatives of the basis (ZOSull(drv = )). For generalized models they are on the link scale. In a simulation (dev/sim-semipred-deriv.R in the package sources) their bands covered the true slope and curvature 0.97-1.00 at the 95% level.

What predictmeans adds

A smoothing program (mgcv::gam(), gamm4) fits these models too. predictmeans adds the mixed-model inference around them.

Means at a covariate value in a designed experiment. Three treatments on the whole plots of six blocks, measured at eleven times; a curve over time for each treatment. The treatment means at time 5 include the curves’ random coefficients, and their comparisons rest on the whole-plot variation, so they have far fewer degrees of freedom than the 180 residual df. predictmeans gives them Satterthwaite df for the combination of fixed and smooth coefficients (as SAS does for estimates that involve random effects), from the variance components of the design with the smoothing parameters held at their estimates; normal-based tests would reject a true null hypothesis too often (dev/sim-semireg-df.R in the package sources), and normal bands of the curves cover 0.91 instead of 0.95 (dev/sim-semipred-df.R).

set.seed(11)
sp <- expand.grid(t = 0:10, A = factor(c("a1", "a2", "a3")), Block = factor(1:6))
sp$y <- 2 + sin(sp$t/2) + c(0, 0.5, 1)[sp$A]*sp$t/10 + rnorm(6, 0, 0.5)[sp$Block] +
  rnorm(18, 0, 0.4)[interaction(sp$Block, sp$A)] + rnorm(nrow(sp), 0, 0.3)
fsp <- semireg(y ~ s(t, by = A, bs = "os", k = 6) + (1 | Block) + (1 | Block:A),
               data = sp)
pm <- predictmeans(fsp, "A", covariate = c(t = 5), pairwise = TRUE)

Which differences between curves are needed? In s(x, f1*f2) the curves are a common curve plus deviation curves for f1, f2 and f1:f2, each with its own variance. anova() tests each variance (the likelihood ratio against its boundary distribution, as varcomp(fit, test = TRUE)) and varcomp() shows their sizes. Here the shapes differ by f1 only:

set.seed(1)
dd <- expand.grid(x = seq(0, 1, length = 25), f1 = factor(c("p", "q")),
                  f2 = factor(c("u", "v", "w")), rep = 1:4)
dd$id <- interaction(dd$f1, dd$f2, dd$rep)
dd$y <- sin(2*pi*dd$x) + (dd$f1 == "q")*1.5*sin(pi*dd$x)^2 +
  rnorm(nlevels(dd$id), 0, 0.2)[dd$id] + rnorm(nrow(dd), 0, 0.25)
fx <- semireg(y ~ s(x, f1*f2, bs = "os", k = 8) + (1 | id), data = dd)
anova(fx)$smooth[c("term", "variance", "LRT", "p.value")]
#>        term     variance        LRT      p.value
#> 1       s_x 0.3163464627  5.7060123 8.453451e-03

The test is conservative: in 200 simulated data sets like these, with no f2 or f1:f2 differences, it rejected at the 5% level in 1-3% of them, and it found the f1 difference in all (dev/sim-semireg-lrt.R). permlmer() on fx$semer gives a permutation test when a level closer to 5% matters.

Counts: rate ratios between curves. semireg_tmb() fits the same smooths with glmmTMB, so a negative binomial with zero inflation is available. contr gives the difference of two curves on the log scale and as a ratio, with a simultaneous band and the ranges where the ratio differs from 1:

From 1.x

smZ() and smoothZ were removed in 2.0: vignette("migrating") shows each 1.x form beside its formula form (they give the same fits) and the 1.x models of the overview article beside their 2.0 code. Other changes since 1.x: the optimizer is nloptwrap (control = lmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa") gives 1.x’s), semipred() limits are 95% (1.x: 90%), anova(fit) replaces lmerTest, and generalized fits include the uncertainty of the smoothing parameters in their bands.

More

  • ?semireg, ?semipred; semireg_tmb() fits the same models with glmmTMB, which also allows negative binomial and other families.
  • The worked examples on the package website use larger data sets (apartment prices, school scores, respiratory infections, pollen counts and a spatial smooth of copper grades).
