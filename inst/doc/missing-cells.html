Missing cells and non-estimable means

A predicted mean is a linear combination of the model’s coefficients. When some treatment combinations have no data, or two factors are confounded, some of these combinations cannot be estimated from the data: emmeans prints them as nonEst and SAS as Non-est. This vignette shows what predictmeans does in these cases and how to get means and comparisons that are estimable.

In short:

  • Cell means of combinations with no data are not shown.
  • A mean that averages over an empty cell (e.g. the mean of a nitrogen level over the varieties when one variety lacks that level) keeps the rule of predictmeans 1.x: it is the average of the observed cells, with a warning naming it.
  • Its comparisons with other means are made on the cells both means have (column over of pairs), not as the difference of two averages over different cells.
  • The table says so: column over of means names the cells such a mean averages. Nothing read off the means can contradict the pair tests: when its pairs are not the differences of the shown means, such a mean has no comparison interval, letter_ci takes the letters from the p-values, and autoplot() draws no comparison or half-LSD bar for it (an open point marks every such mean).
  • A cell is a combination of the factors of one interaction term of the model. Factors that do not interact do not make cells together: a combination of them without data is not an empty cell, because an additive model can estimate it.
  • Confounded factors (two factors that do not interact in the model and do not occur in all combinations, so that their effects cannot be told apart): the mean over all their combinations does not exist. The means average over the combinations that exist, with a warning that names the two factors and says how to state the structure in the model.
  • Means that still cannot be estimated are NA, with a message. Their pairwise differences are given when they can be estimated, and letters among those.
  • The means table has an estimable column and the number of observations, n.
library(predictmeans)

An empty cell

Suppose the plots of Victory at the highest nitrogen level were lost in the oats split plot:

Oats <- as.data.frame(nlme::Oats)
Oats$N <- factor(Oats$nitro)
Om <- subset(Oats, !(Variety == "Victory" & N == "0.6"))
with(Om, table(Variety, N))
#>              N
#> Variety       0 0.2 0.4 0.6
#>   Golden Rain 6   6   6   6
#>   Marvellous  6   6   6   6
#>   Victory     6   6   6   0
fm <- lmer(yield ~ N * Variety + (1 | Block/Variety), data = Om)
#> fixed-effect model matrix is rank deficient so dropping 1 column / coefficient

The model with the interaction has one coefficient fewer (lme4 drops it). The cell means are those of the 11 combinations with data:

pm_cells <- predictmeans(fm, "N:Variety")
nrow(pm_cells$means)
#> [1] 11

The nitrogen means average over the varieties. For N = 0.6 only two varieties have data, so its mean is their average, and predictmeans warns:

pm_n <- predictmeans(fm, "N")
#> Warning: No data for N:Variety 0.6:Victory. The mean of N = 0.6 averages only
#> its observed cells; its pairs use the cells both means have (column 'over').
#> See vignette("missing-cells").
pm_n$means[, c("N", "over", "estimate", "std.error", "n")]
#>     N                            over  estimate std.error  n
#> 1   0                            <NA>  79.38889  6.972348 18
#> 2 0.2                            <NA>  98.88889  6.972348 18
#> 3 0.4                            <NA> 114.22222  6.972348 18
#> 4 0.6 Variety Golden Rain, Marvellous 125.83333  7.471667 12

That mean leaves out Victory, the lowest-yielding variety at the other nitrogen levels, so it is too high compared with the others; emmeans calls it non-estimable. The difference 125.8 − 79.4 = 46.4 would mix the nitrogen effect with the variety effect (Victory is in the mean of N = 0 but not in that of 0.6). So predictmeans compares N = 0.6 with each other level on the varieties both have, Golden Rain and Marvellous (column over): 0.6 − 0 is the average of their two differences, 42.5. Pairs among the complete levels are the differences of the means as usual:

pm_n2 <- suppressWarnings(predictmeans(fm, "N", pairwise = TRUE))
pm_n2$pairs[, c("level1", "level2", "over", "estimate", "std.error", "p.value")]

Comparing the two averages instead would wrongly call 0.6 and 0.4 different here (p = 0.03 against 0.08 on the common varieties). In simulations (dev/review-defaults.md in the package sources) comparing averages over different cells rejected 8–78% of true null hypotheses when the other factor had an effect; comparing on the common cells 4–6%. When two means have no cell in common their difference is NA (over = "none"). pm_joint() tests a term on the cells common to all its means, and contrastmeans() compares on the cells the means of each contrast have in common. anova() does the same for its Type III tests: the interaction keeps the contrasts that use no empty cell (5 df here, not 6), and the main effects are the pm_joint() tests:

The mean of N = 0.6 stays in the table, labelled by over, because it is the honest estimate of its own quantity (the average over the varieties that have it). A table of means over the cells common to all levels would not be better: it would change the means of complete levels whenever another level loses a cell, and with scattered empty cells the cells common to all can shrink to one or none. Readings that treat the means as comparable are switched off for it instead: no comparison interval (its pairs are on common cells), letters from the p-values with letter_ci = TRUE, an open point in autoplot():

ggplot2::autoplot(pm_n2, interval = "comparison")

Other comparisons that stay within the observed cells are sound too, e.g. nitrogen levels within each variety:

pm_v <- predictmeans(fm, "N:Variety", atvar = "Variety", adj = "tukey")

What counts as a cell

A cell is a combination of the factors of one interaction term. In y ~ date/farm + date*trt the cells are date × farm and date × treatment; farm and treatment do not interact. Suppose treatment B has no data on farm F2 at date d2:

set.seed(11)
dft <- expand.grid(rep = 1:3, trt = factor(c("A", "B")), farm = factor(c("F1", "F2")),
                   date = factor(c("d1", "d2")))
dft <- dft[!(dft$date == "d2" & dft$farm == "F2" & dft$trt == "B"), ]
dft$y <- 5 + as.integer(dft$farm) * as.integer(dft$date) + 0.5 * (dft$trt == "B") +
  rnorm(nrow(dft), 0, 0.3)
fd <- lm(y ~ date/farm + date*trt, data = dft)
predictmeans(fd, "date:trt")$means[, c("date", "trt", "estimate", "n")]
#>   date trt estimate n
#> 1   d1   A 6.491091 6
#> 2   d1   B 6.835039 6
#> 3   d2   A 7.756290 6
#> 4   d2   B 8.618384 3

Every date × farm and every date × treatment cell has data, and the model says the treatment effect is the same on both farms, so the mean of B at d2 over both farms can be estimated: no empty cell, no warning. (An average over farm F1 only would make B look worse than A just because F1 is the poorer farm.) With a farm × treatment interaction in the model (date/farm*trt) the same combination is an empty cell, and the rule of the first section applies.

Confounded factors

In this spray trial the highest spray volume was used only with fine droplets, and the other two volumes only with medium droplets:

set.seed(3)
spray <- data.frame(
  droplet = factor(rep(c("medium", "fine"), c(40, 10)), levels = c("medium", "fine")),
  vol = factor(c(rep(c("130", "200"), 20), rep("400", 10))),
  conc = factor(rep(c("none", "half"), 25)))
spray$y <- 5 + 0.3 * (spray$conc == "half") - 0.4 * (spray$droplet == "fine") +
  rnorm(50, sd = 0.2)
with(spray, table(droplet, vol))
#>         vol
#> droplet  130 200 400
#>   medium  20  20   0
#>   fine     0   0  10

In a model with droplet and vol as separate factors, the effect of volume 400 cannot be told apart from that of fine droplets. A volume mean averaged over both droplet sizes needs both, so it cannot be estimated. predictmeans() then averages over the droplet–volume combinations that exist and says so:

fs <- lm(y ~ droplet + conc + vol, data = spray)
pv <- predictmeans(fs, "vol", pairwise = TRUE)
#> Warning: 'droplet' and 'vol' are confounded in the data: the means average over
#> the combinations that exist (to say so: droplet/vol in the formula, leave one
#> factor out, or weights = "cells"). No data for droplet:vol fine:130, fine:200,
#> medium:400. The means of vol = 130, 200, 400 average only their observed cells;
#> their pairs use the cells both means have (column 'over'). See
#> vignette("missing-cells").
pv$means[, c("vol", "estimate", "std.error", "n", "group")]
#>   vol estimate  std.error  n group
#> 1 130 5.030309 0.06844537 20    A 
#> 2 200 5.246957 0.06844537 20    A 
#> 3 400 4.731546 0.05588541 10     B

Read these means with the warning in mind: “volume 400” is “volume 400 with fine droplets”, and its difference from volume 130 is also a difference between droplet sizes. The data cannot separate the two.

Three ways to say what you want

The warning names three ways to get the means without relying on the fallback. They are not the same mean:

Way What the mean is
Write the nesting in the formula (droplet/vol) equal-weight average over the combinations that exist; the same numbers as the fallback, without the warning about confounding
Leave out the factor that adds nothing (when one factor is determined by the other, as a farm by its pastures) equal-weight average over the levels that remain; the same numbers again
weights = "cells" average over the observed combinations weighted by their numbers of observations; depends on how the data are spread over the other factors

The first two change the formula, not the fit: the fitted values and the likelihood are the same. Stating the structure in the model is the better record of the design:

fs2 <- lm(y ~ conc + droplet/vol, data = spray)
pn <- predictmeans(fs2, "vol")
#> 'vol' is not a term of the model: its factors do not interact there, so these means are the sums of their effects (the differences between the levels of one factor are the same at every level of the others).
cbind(pn$means[, c("vol", "estimate")], fallback = pv$means$estimate,
      cells = predictmeans(fs, "vol", weights = "cells")$means$estimate)
#>   vol estimate fallback    cells
#> 1 130 5.030309 5.030309 4.987406
#> 2 200 5.246957 5.246957 5.289860
#> 3 400 4.731546 4.731546 4.731546

Confounded within another factor

Confounding is not always nesting over the whole data set. In a grazing trial sampled on several dates, each pasture type is on one farm at a date, but a pasture type can be on another farm at a later date:

set.seed(5)
past <- rbind(expand.grid(date = c("d1", "d2"), pasture = c("P1", "P2", "P3", "P4"),
                          trt = c("A", "B"), rep = 1:3),
              expand.grid(date = "d3", pasture = c("P1", "P4"), trt = c("A", "B"), rep = 1:3))
past$farm <- ifelse(past$date == "d3", ifelse(past$pasture == "P1", "F3", "F4"),
                    ifelse(past$pasture == "P4", "F2", "F1"))
for (v in c("date", "pasture", "trt", "farm")) past[[v]] <- factor(past[[v]])
past$y <- as.integer(past$date) + as.integer(past$pasture)/2 + 0.5*(past$trt == "B") +
  rnorm(nrow(past), 0, 0.3)
fp <- lm(y ~ date/farm + date/(trt + pasture)^2, data = past)
pp <- predictmeans(fp, "date:trt")
#> Warning: 'farm' and 'pasture' are confounded in the data: the means average
#> over the combinations that exist (to say so: farm/pasture in the formula, leave
#> one factor out, or weights = "cells"). No data for date:farm d3:F1, d3:F2,
#> d1:F3, d2:F3, d1:F4, d2:F4; date:trt:pasture d3:A:P2, d3:B:P2, d3:A:P3,
#> d3:B:P3; farm:pasture F2:P1, F4:P1, F2:P2, F3:P2, F4:P2, F2:P3, .... The means
#> of date:trt = d1:A, d1:B, d2:A, d2:B, d3:A, d3:B average only their observed
#> cells; their pairs use the cells both means have (column 'over'). See
#> vignette("missing-cells").

Farm and pasture do not interact in the model, and at each date the farm is known from the pasture: the farm terms add nothing to the fit (their coefficients are aliased). The package finds the pair of factors by trying: it leaves out the combinations without data of one pair at a time (first pairs among the factors averaged over) and keeps the first choice with which every mean can be estimated. The means equal those of the model without farm:

fp0 <- lm(y ~ date/(trt + pasture)^2, data = past)
p0 <- suppressWarnings(predictmeans(fp0, "date:trt"))
cbind(pp$means[, c("date", "trt", "estimate")], without_farm = p0$means$estimate)
#>   date trt estimate without_farm
#> 1   d1   A 2.301947     2.301947
#> 2   d1   B 2.770451     2.770451
#> 3   d2   A 3.236178     3.236178
#> 4   d2   B 3.792431     3.792431
#> 5   d3   A 4.263867     4.263867
#> 6   d3   B 4.789150     4.789150

(The remaining warning for fp0 is about date d3, where two pasture types were not sampled: an ordinary empty cell.)

Before this rule (decided 2026-11-16) such means were NA; in predictmeans 1.x they were the same averages, without a warning that said why.

Checklist

  • Look at the n and estimable columns of the means table, and at warnings and messages.
  • Compare means that average over the same cells; use atvar or contrasts within the observed cells when some are empty.
  • When the warning says two factors are confounded, the means are over the combinations that exist. Write the structure into the model (nesting, or leave out the factor that adds nothing) so that the model says what the means are; weights = "cells" gives a different, frequency-weighted mean.
